A geological survey conducted in 1874 identified a rich silver vein running through the hills west of Thornwick Valley, at depths ranging from 50 to 300 feet below the surface. The survey, commissioned by prospector Wren Thornwick, led directly to the founding of the Thornwick Mining Company the following year. The vein was estimated to contain enough ore to support at least sixty years of continuous mining, a prediction that proved accurate when the mine finally closed in 1936.
